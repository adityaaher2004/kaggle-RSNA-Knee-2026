# Task: Design a State-of-the-Art Model for the RSNA Knee Abnormality Detection Competition

You are a senior researcher in **medical computer vision, MRI analysis, multimodal learning, weakly supervised learning, and competitive ML**, with expertise in PyTorch, MONAI, DICOM, 2D/3D vision transformers, multiple-instance learning, knowledge distillation, and medical vision-language models.

Your task is to design a **high-performance, computationally realistic model and complete training system** for:

**RSNA Knee Abnormality Detection**

Do not produce a generic medical-imaging architecture. Design specifically for the structure and constraints of this competition.

---

## 1. Competition formulation

The dataset consists of knee MRI studies.

Each training study contains:

### `train.csv`

One row per `StudyInstanceUID`.

Fields include:

* `StudyInstanceUID`
* `Report`
* `ACL`
* `MCL`
* `Medial Meniscus`
* `Lateral Meniscus`
* `Medial OA`
* `Lateral OA`
* `PF OA`
* `Effusion`
* `Synovitis`
* `Baker's`
* `Contusion`
* `Fracture`

The twelve targets are binary and may simultaneously be positive.

Therefore formulate the task as:

$$
\mathbf y_i\in\{0,1\}^{12}
$$

with twelve study-level predictions.

---

### `train_series.csv`

One row per MRI series.

Each series has:

* `StudyInstanceUID`
* `SeriesInstanceUID`
* `Fluid_Sensitive`
* `Fat_Suppression`
* `Anatomical_Plane`

where `Anatomical_Plane` is:

* Sagittal
* Coronal
* Axial

Each series consists of multiple DICOM slices.

The directory hierarchy is:

```text
train_series/
    StudyInstanceUID/
        SeriesInstanceUID/
            SOPInstanceUID.dcm
```

A study therefore has the hierarchy:

$$
\boxed{
Study
\rightarrow
Series
\rightarrow
Slices
}
$$

---

## 2. Critical train/test modality constraint

Training provides:

$$
\boxed{
MRI + radiology\ Report + 12\ labels
}
$$

but testing provides:

$$
\boxed{
MRI\ only
}
$$

The report is NOT available at test time.

Treat this as a central architectural constraint.

Investigate the problem as:

$$
(X,T,Y)_{\text{train}}
$$

but:

$$
(X)_{\text{test}}.
$$

Consider the relationship to:

* Learning Using Privileged Information (LUPI)
* knowledge distillation
* multimodal teacher → unimodal student learning
* cross-modal representation alignment
* contrastive learning
* auxiliary text supervision
* modality dropout
* missing-modality learning

Do not assume the report can simply be concatenated with MRI features at inference.

```text

 ↓
```

Then design an MRI-only student:

```text

 ↓

 ↓
Student
 ↓
12 predictions
```

Investigate how to transfer knowledge:

### Logit distillation

$$
\mathcal L_{KD}^{logit}
=
KL(p_T^\tau||p_S^\tau).
$$

### Feature distillation

$$
\mathcal L_{KD}^{feature}
=
\|z_T-z_S\|_2^2.
$$

### Contrastive image-text alignment

$$
\mathcal L_{ITC}.
$$

### Label supervision

$$
\mathcal L_{BCE}.
$$

### Attention distillation

Transfer which slices/series the teacher considers diagnostically relevant.

Compare these methods.

# Standards for reasoning

Follow these rules:

1. **Do not assume the most complicated model is the best model.**
2. **Respect the study → series → slice hierarchy.**
3. **Treat the report as privileged training information because it disappears at inference.**
4. **Do not introduce information unavailable at test time.**
5. **Separate clinically plausible reasoning from experimentally validated claims.**
6. **Distinguish established methods from speculative ideas.**
7. **Prefer ablation-tested architectural additions.**
8. **Consider the relatively limited number of studies when selecting model capacity.**
9. **Treat DICOM preprocessing as part of the model rather than an afterthought.**
10. **Optimize for the actual competition metric rather than generic classification accuracy.**
11. **Use recent literature from 2024–2026 when proposing frontier components.**
12. **Cite primary papers whenever possible.**
13. **If a paper's method is being adapted, explicitly explain the adaptation.**
14. **Identify what should be implemented first versus what should only be attempted after a strong baseline exists.**
15. **Be skeptical of claims that attention weights are inherently explanations.**
16. **Do not hallucinate dataset properties that have not been verified.**
17. **If competition rules or dataset details have changed, verify them from current sources.**
